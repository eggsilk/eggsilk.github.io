Services

An installation exposed to CBAM already holds most of the instruments. What tends to be missing is the determination method and the documentation a verifier can follow. Strata prepares four deliverables, in sequence, to the EU Monitoring and Reporting Regulation, Reg. (EU) 2018/2066.

The deliverables

Each deliverable is a named artifact rather than a promised outcome. They build on one another. The monitoring plan sets what is measured and to what tier. The measurement campaign produces the data. The determination turns that data into an embedded-emissions figure, and the dossier assembles the evidence a verifier needs. An engagement can begin at any one of them, though the sequence follows the order the regulation reads in.

01

Monitoring plan

The source streams and emission sources that set the boundary, the method and tier per stream, and the data-flow and control procedures. This is the document required for approval under MRR Articles 12 and 14, Annex I.

Read the deliverable
02

Measurement campaign

Existing instrumentation brought into a clean data flow, together with the carbon-specific analyses a plant's QC lab does not produce (biogenic fraction, non-carbonate carbon, and the calcination of the dust that leaves the kiln), run through ISO 17025 laboratories.

Read the deliverable
03

Emissions determination

Calcination, combustion, and process CO₂ computed by the mass-balance or standard method, attributed per good and per CN code across the reporting period, with the uncertainty budget stated per source stream.

Read the deliverable
04

Verification dossier

Method, data trail, QA, and uncertainty assembled into the evidence an accredited verifier needs under the AVR (EU) 2018/2067. The dossier is built toward that handoff from the first day of monitoring.

Read the deliverable

The standards applied

The EU Monitoring and Reporting Regulation, Reg. (EU) 2018/2066, governs how emissions are monitored and reported; the AVR, Reg. (EU) 2018/2067, governs their verification. Carbon-specific analyses are run to the relevant EN and ISO methods, among them EN ISO 13833 (biogenic carbon by ¹⁴C), EN 13137 (organic carbon), and ISO 17025 (laboratory competence). The named standard for each deliverable is stated on its own page.
