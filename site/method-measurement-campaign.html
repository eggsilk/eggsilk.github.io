Measurement campaign

The plan's required tiers become measurements. The campaign also adds three carbon-specific analyses a plant's QC lab is not built to produce.

What the campaign measures

A modern installation already owns most of the instruments that emissions monitoring needs. Those instruments were built to control product chemistry and fuel value rather than to close a carbon balance. The campaign brings them into one reconciled data flow and adds the analyses that are missing.

  • Activity data, from instruments already on site. Weigh feeders, weighbridge, and metering come into a single reconciled data flow at the tier the plan requires.
  • The biogenic fraction of the fuel mix. Radiocarbon determines it (EN ISO 13833 on stack CO₂, or EN ISO 21644 on the solid fuel), because qualifying biomass carries a zero emission factor and a calorimeter measures energy rather than carbon age.
  • The non-carbonate (organic) carbon in the raw meal. Total carbon minus inorganic carbon by EN 13137 or DIN 19539 gives a process-emission term that combustion accounting and calcination accounting both miss.
  • The calcination of the kiln dust. The degree of calcination of cement kiln and bypass dust is fixed from paired loss-on-ignition, so the CO₂ that leaves with the dust is not lost from an output-based calculation.

How it is run

  • Through accredited laboratories. Carbon-specific analyses run in ISO 17025 laboratories. The practice designs sampling for the bale-to-bale heterogeneity of alternative fuels rather than a single grab sample.
  • Where the uncertainty actually sits. Effort goes to representative sampling of variable streams, which dominates total uncertainty far more than a feeder's metering error.
  • Traceable to the plan. Every measurement traces back to the source stream and tier the monitoring plan defined, so the campaign is auditable against an approved baseline.

The deliverable

The campaign produces a measurement dataset with documented sampling and analysis methods, together with the accredited laboratory reports behind it. These are the measured inputs the emissions determination is computed from. The reasoning behind the three analyses is set out in a working note, the three carbon analyses a cement plant's QC lab does not produce.

Prepared to the EU Monitoring and Reporting Regulation, Reg. (EU) 2018/2066; carbon analyses to EN ISO 13833 and EN ISO 21644 (biogenic ¹⁴C), EN 13137 and DIN 19539 (organic carbon), under ISO 17025 laboratory accreditation.
