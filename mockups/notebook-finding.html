Is CO₂ bound in urea deductible from embedded emissions?

Date2026-09-01
StatusDraft — citation pass pending before publication

CitesReg. (EU) 2023/956; the 2025 implementing and delegated acts [exact citations under review]
Revisionsnone

Question

In urea production, a substantial share of the CO₂ generated upstream in ammonia synthesis is chemically bound into the product. During CBAM's transitional period it was widely assumed this bound carbon would reduce the embedded emissions of urea. Does the definitive-period law allow that deduction?

Short answer

No. The definitive-period calculation rules admit a deduction only for CO₂ permanently bound in listed products — and the list covers mineral carbonates in construction products. Urea is not on it. The CO₂ leaves the plant in the product and returns to the atmosphere on application; the law treats it as emitted.

Analysis

The deduction question turns on a single annex, not on chemistry. The calculation act for the definitive period citation pending permits subtracting CO₂ that is permanently chemically bound in a product, on the condition that the product appears in the annexed list of eligible uses. That list names precipitated calcium carbonate and comparable mineral carbonates incorporated into construction products — categories chosen for permanence of storage.

Urea fails the permanence test by design: the bound CO₂ is released when the fertiliser hydrolyses in soil, within days to weeks of application. The transitional-period practice of netting it off therefore does not carry over. For a declarant importing urea, embedded emissions must be calculated without the deduction, which materially raises the declared figure relative to transitional-period expectations — on the order of the stoichiometric CO₂ content of the product itself, roughly 0.73 t CO₂ per tonne of urea figure to be re-derived for publication.

Two practical consequences for 2027 declarations:

  • Producer-side monitoring reports prepared on transitional assumptions will understate embedded emissions for urea and downstream nitrogen fertilisers; the roll-up needs re-examination before verified figures are relied on.
  • Default values for the fertiliser sector carry a flat mark-up in the definitive regime, so the verified-versus-default decision changes shape for importers who assumed the deduction.

Limits

This finding addresses the definitive period only; it takes no position on transitional-period reporting already filed. It does not consider carbon captured and geologically stored (a separate deduction with its own conditions), and it does not address national fertiliser regulation.
