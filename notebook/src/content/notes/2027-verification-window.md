Three published facts, read together, define the first verification cycle of the CBAM definitive regime. The first declaration deadline is fixed in law. The earliest possible start of verification is fixed by how emissions reports work. And the pool of people permitted to do the verifying is still being accredited. This note sets out the three facts and states the conclusion I draw from them.

## Nine months, fixed at both ends

The far end is statutory. Under Regulation (EU) 2023/956 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/2083, the first annual CBAM declaration, covering 2026 imports, with a verification report attached and certificates surrendered, is due by **30 September 2027**.<sup class="sn-ref">1</sup><span class="sn" data-n="1">The Omnibus amendment (2025/2083) moved this from 31 May. The extension is already in the deadline.</span>

The near end follows from the shape of the data. An annual emissions report can only be verified once the year it covers is complete. The German Emissions Trading Authority (DEHSt) confirmed the consequence in January 2026, in comments reported by Argus: no CBAM verification takes place in 2026, because only the complete, verified emissions report for the calendar year is valid. Verification of 2026 data therefore runs from **1 January 2027 to 30 September 2027**.

Nine months, for the entire first-year workload of the regime.

## A verifier pool that is still being accredited

Who may do the verifying is set by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551: verifiers accredited by a national accreditation body against the harmonised standard **EN ISO/IEC 17029:2019**, with Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2546 governing how the verification itself is performed. Both acts apply from 1 January 2026, which means the accreditation of the pool could only begin this year.

It has begun, and no more than that. DAkkS, the German accreditation body, opened CBAM accreditation applications on 23 February 2026, with a streamlined scope extension for existing EU ETS verifiers. The European co-operation for Accreditation stood up its CBAM task force at the beginning of March 2026 and published the directory of national bodies intending to offer CBAM accreditation on 18 March 2026. As of early July 2026, **no CBAM verifier had been accredited**. Published expectations put the first accreditations in the autumn of 2026 (commentators) to the end of 2026 (DAkkS, for its own procedures). Those are expectations, not deadlines; nothing in the acts compels the pool to exist by a given date.

Against that pool stands the demand side. Some 403 verification bodies hold EU ETS accreditation in the Union Registry and could extend scope; 4,100 CBAM declarants were authorised on 1 January 2026, out of roughly 12,000 applications filed in the first week of the year.<sup class="sn-ref">2</sup><span class="sn" data-n="2">Figures compiled by Ojha and Raina in May 2026 from registry and Commission data. An estimate, attributed as such.</span> Each declarant may need verified data from several third-country installations. The French competent authority has warned publicly that verification capacity may fall short before September 2027. These are estimates and warnings rather than official projections, and they all point the same way.

## The first year is the most demanding by rule

The compressed window would matter less if first-year verification were light. It is the opposite, by design. Under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2546, the first verification of an installation requires a **physical site visit in all cases**. A virtual visit is permitted only once a physical visit has taken place in the immediately preceding reporting period, and only under risk conditions set out in Article 3; Article 4 admits substitution solely in serious, extraordinary and unforeseeable circumstances. Every installation entering the regime, which in 2027 is every installation, must be walked in person.

So the nine months are not desk months. They are travel to third-country installations, scheduled against every other declarant's identical deadline, by a pool whose size will only become known in the months before the window opens.

> A verifier arriving in January 2027 can check a year of records. No one can measure 2026 again.

## What follows for 2026

I draw one conclusion: **the work that decides whether verification succeeds in 2027 is being done, or not done, now.** The data the verifier will check is 2026 data. A monitoring plan that was not in place, a fuel stream that was not sampled, a dust bleed whose calcination degree was never measured: none of this can be reconstructed in January 2027. The verifier checks records; records are made in the year they describe.

Concretely, an installation that intends to carry verified actual values into its customers' first declarations needs four things running before the year ends: a monitoring plan consistent with the calculation rules of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2547; activity data metered and logged continuously, not assembled retrospectively; the carbon-specific analyses (biogenic fraction, non-carbonate carbon, dust calcination) commissioned within the reporting year; and the data trail assembled so a first-time auditor can trace the figure to its source documents. A fifth follows when the pool forms: engagement with a verification body as accreditations land, since queue position in a nine-month window is itself a variable.

The alternative is the default values, at a 10% mark-up on country-average intensity for 2026 cement, iron and steel, and aluminium imports, rising to 30% by 2028.<sup class="sn-ref">3</sup><span class="sn" data-n="3">Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621, Annex I. Fertilisers carry a flat 1% mark-up.</span> For a producer whose actual emissions sit below the country average, defaults concede the entire difference plus the mark-up.

Nothing published between January and July 2026 widened the window. No accreditation had been granted; the declaration module of the CBAM registry had not opened as of DEHSt's March update; and the regime is extending rather than pausing, with the Council agreeing its position on 12 June 2026 to bring downstream steel and aluminium products into scope from 2028. The window stays nine months wide, and it is approached from one side only.
